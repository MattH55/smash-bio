What this category is

  Simulations, labelled as such

Every record carries the source publication or dataset, the simulated system, the model family and force field, the pulling protocol and geometry, and the reported results. They have their own record type (SIM-######), their own pages and downloads, and they never stand in for an experiment.

  Units are part of the data

Coarse-grained models often report reduced units. Each value keeps the unit the source wrote plus a unit_system field, and a reduced value is only converted when the paper states the conversion — the conversion is stored with it.

  Matched to experiments

Simulations are matched to SMASH-BIO and MechanoProDB experiments by shared resolver-checked identifiers and the same pulling geometry. Each link carries a match level, so a loose match is never presented as a like-for-like one.

How a simulation is compared with an experiment

The API and the downloads keep both sides of every link, and the disagreement is the interesting part.

Coverage

1 · Same construct, same geometry

A shared resolver-checked identifier, the same pulling geometry in the experimental vocabulary, and constructs that overlap. This is the level at which a force can be compared directly.

2 · Same molecule, other geometry

The molecule matches, but the construct or the geometry differs or is not stated. Comparable with care, and never plotted on a shared physical axis without a stated conversion.

3 · Homolog

No shared identifier: the link rests on a distinctive molecule-name token. Weakest level, shown as such, and useful only as a pointer.
